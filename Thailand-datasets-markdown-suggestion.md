#### About PALSAR-2
PALSAR-2, aboard JAXA's ALOS-2 satellite, is an L-band Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR): it emits its own microwave signal and measures the reflection, so it can image the ground day or night, through cloud cover, and even partially through vegetation canopy. Shown here is the *HH (co-polarized) channel*, which is generally more sensitive to surface structure and moisture than to vegetation itself.

 The next steps show instead the *HV (cross-polarized) channel*, temporally aggregated by growth season. HV backscatter is driven by volume scattering and tends to respond more strongly to canopy structure and biomass, making it the more informative band for tracking rice growth through the season. Rather than single acquisition dates, each following composite is a per-pixel median of all available scenes within a given rice growth stage. This averages out day-to-day noise while still capturing how backscatter shifts from one stage to the next.

#### Seedling Stage (1 May – 6 Jun 2025)
The cycle begins here: fields around Suphan Buri have just been transplanted, and canopy cover is minimal. This composite, drawn from ALOS-2 PALSAR-2 ScanSAR HV backscatter, marks the baseline against which the later growth stages can be compared.

#### Panicle Development (14 Jul – 19 Aug 2025)
Canopy structure has thickened considerably by this stage, as the crop develops its panicles. HV backscatter, driven by volume scattering, tends to strengthen as biomass and canopy complexity increase compared to the seedling stage.

#### Flowering Stage (20 Aug – 25 Sep 2025)

#### Harvesting Stage (26 Sep – 31 Oct 2025)
By harvest, crops have dried and ripened, changing both moisture content and structure. The study's own results found that Sentinel-1 VV backscatter combined with dry biomass at this stage was the single strongest predictor of rice yield in Suphan Buri. This makes the ALOS-2 view here a useful point of comparison for how L-band responds at the same moment in the crop cycle.
