#### About PALSAR-2
PALSAR-2, aboard JAXA's ALOS-2 satellite, is an L-band Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR): it emits its own microwave signal and measures the reflection, so it can image the ground day or night, through cloud cover, and even partially through vegetation canopy. Shown here is the *HH (co-polarized) channel*, which is generally more sensitive to surface structure and moisture than to vegetation itself.

The next steps show instead the *HV (cross-polarized) channel* for three single ALOS-2 acquisition dates matched to rice growth stages in 2025: seedling (10 May), panicle development (17 July) and harvesting (20 August). Each image is a single observation rather than an average. No ALOS-2 observations were available for the tillering stage (4 to 25 June), so that stage is not shown. HV backscatter is driven by volume scattering and tends to respond more strongly to canopy structure and biomass than HH, which makes it the more informative band for following rice growth through the season.

#### Seedling Stage (10 May 2025)
The cycle begins here: fields around Suphan Buri have just been transplanted, and canopy cover is minimal. This single acquisition, drawn from ALOS-2 PALSAR-2 ScanSAR HV backscatter, marks the baseline against which the later growth stages can be compared.

#### Panicle Development (17 Jul 2025)
By mid-July the canopy has thickened considerably as the crop develops its panicles. HV backscatter, driven by volume scattering, tends to strengthen as biomass and canopy complexity increase compared to the seedling stage, though moisture and irrigation conditions in the fields can also influence the signal.

#### Harvesting Stage (20 Aug 2025)
By harvest, crops have dried and ripened, changing both moisture content and structure. The study's own results found that Sentinel-1 VV backscatter combined with dry biomass at this stage was the single strongest predictor of rice yield in Suphan Buri. This makes the ALOS-2 view here a useful point of comparison for how L-band responds at the same moment in the crop cycle.
